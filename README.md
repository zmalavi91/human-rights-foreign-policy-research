# Doctoral Research

## Thesis Title

**Human Rights in International Relations: Role of USA and the European Union in Post-Cold War Era**

## Institution

Department of Political Science  
Aligarh Muslim University, Aligarh, India

## Research Period

May 2019 – June 2023

## Research Area

Human Rights and International Relations

## Central Research Problem

The doctoral research examined how the United States and the European Union shaped, interpreted, promoted, and implemented human-rights norms in the post-Cold War international environment.

It investigated the relationship between:

- Human-rights principles.
- Foreign-policy objectives.
- International institutions.
- Major-power interests.
- Multilateral mechanisms.
- Geopolitical transformation.
- International norm development.

## Research Context

The post-Cold War period involved major changes in:

- The structure of international power.
- The role of the United States.
- The external identity of the European Union.
- Human-rights diplomacy.
- Multilateral institutions.
- International intervention.
- Global governance.
- International accountability.

The research examined whether human-rights policies were applied consistently across different international contexts or whether they were shaped by strategic interests, political priorities, and changing geopolitical conditions.

# Conceptual Framework

## Human Rights

Human rights are understood as universal claims connected with human dignity, equality, liberty, justice, and protection from arbitrary power.

## Foreign Policy

Foreign policy refers to the strategies, decisions, practices, and instruments through which states and political entities engage with the external environment.

## Human-Rights Governance

Human-rights governance includes:

- International norms.
- Institutions.
- Treaty systems.
- Monitoring mechanisms.
- Diplomatic practices.
- Accountability procedures.
- Civil-society participation.
- State obligations.
- International cooperation.

## Global Power Dynamics

Global power dynamics refer to the distribution and exercise of political, economic, military, institutional, and ideological power in the international system.

## Norm Evolution

Norm evolution refers to the process through which standards of appropriate behaviour emerge, change, spread, become institutionalized, or face contestation.

## Selectivity and Inconsistency

A central analytical concern is whether powerful states apply human-rights principles consistently or selectively according to strategic interests and geopolitical circumstances.
