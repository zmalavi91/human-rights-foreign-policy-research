# Conceptual Framework

## Human Rights

Human rights are understood as universal claims connected with human dignity, equality, liberty, justice, and protection from arbitrary power.

## Foreign Policy

Foreign policy refers to the strategies, decisions, practices, and instruments through which states engage with the external environment.

## Human-Rights Governance

Human-rights governance includes international norms, institutions, treaty systems, monitoring mechanisms, diplomatic practices, accountability procedures, and civil-society participation.

## Global Power Dynamics

Global power dynamics refer to the distribution and exercise of political, economic, military, institutional, and ideological power in international politics.

## Norm Evolution

Norm evolution refers to the emergence, transformation, institutionalization, contestation, and diffusion of standards of appropriate international behaviour.

## Selectivity

Selectivity refers to the possibility that powerful states may apply human-rights principles inconsistently according to strategic, political, or geopolitical circumstances.

## Analytical Relationship

The research examines how human-rights norms are affected by:

- Foreign-policy interests.
- International institutions.
- Major-power rivalry.
- Security concerns.
- Strategic intervention.
- Diplomatic pressure.
- Domestic political considerations.
- Global public opinion.
- Accountability mechanisms.
